# Evidence behind the method

Checked by web search in October 2026. Much of the AI-tutor research is recent, small or still a preprint, so treat it as directional. Spacing effects are real but smaller in real courses than in lab studies.

| Principle | Where it shows up | Source | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Retrieval practice | Do now, exit tickets, checkpoints | Roediger & Karpicke 2006; Dunlosky et al. 2013 ("high utility"); Yang et al. 2021 classroom meta-analysis, g ≈ 0.50 | Strongest single technique |
| Spacing | Review cards | Cepeda et al. 2006; Murray, Horner & Göbel 2025 meta-analysis, g ≈ 0.28 | Smaller in real courses than in labs |
| Even vs expanding gaps | Short box intervals | Latimier et al. 2021; Kang et al. 2014 | No reliable edge for expanding; the number of retrievals matters more |
| Interleaving | Mixed do-now, cumulative checkpoints | Brunmair & Richter 2019 | Depends on the material; weaker on first exposure to complex content |
| Pretesting and generation | S1 pretest, predictions | Richland, Kornell & Kao 2009; Slamecka & Graf 1978 | Wrong guesses still help |
| Self-explanation | Explain back | Chi et al. 1994 | |
| Learning by teaching | Teach sessions | Mollick & Mollick 2023 ("AI as student") | |
| Worked examples, then fading | First instance of each new kind of code | Sweller; Renkl & Atkinson | |
| Small steps, daily review, ~80% success | Session shape | Rosenshine, *Principles of Instruction*, 2012 | |
| Mastery learning | Checkpoint pass bar | Bloom 1968 | |
| Desirable difficulties | No notes, cold answers | Bjork 1994 | |
| Dual coding | Draw sessions | Paivio; Mayer | |
| Contingent scaffolding | Fade help based on what they do | Wood, Bruner & Ross 1976; 2026 adaptive AI tutor work (arXiv 2609.22993) | Slow and Socratic helps newcomers most; fast pacing hurt retention |
| Unguarded AI hurts unaided performance | Hint-only lane, solo reps | Bastani et al., PNAS 2025 (about 17% lower on unaided exam); Stanford SCALE K-12 evidence review 2026 | Students prefer the more helpful, less effective version |
| Hint-only tutor design | Hint ladder, immediate feedback | Kestin & Miller, *Scientific Reports* 2025 (Harvard physics RCT, n=194, >2x gains); Harvard CS50 Duck | Seven principles: active thinking, limited load, small steps, accurate info, immediate feedback, self-pacing, growth mindset |
| Two lanes | No-AI reps prove it; open lanes for learning | University of Sydney two-lane policy (2025) | |
| AI use levels | The three lanes | AI Assessment Scale, Perkins, Furze et al. 2024 | Collapsed from 5 levels to 3 for less friction |
| Oral defense | Code viva | UK university viva pilots 2025–26; viva simulator (arXiv 2511.05530) | Small-scale so far |
| AI roles | Tutor, simulator, student | Mollick & Mollick, "Assigning AI," 2023 | The tutor role's main risk is confident errors, so verify before teaching |
